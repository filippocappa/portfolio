## The problem

Most training plans are written once and then followed. By the third week they no longer match what the runner has actually done: a missed session, a cold, a race that went better than expected. Apps accept any goal time without asking whether it's realistic, and a human coach costs more than most amateur runners will pay.

I'm one of those runners, and I wanted a plan that reacts to what actually happened.

## How it works

Roen connects to Strava and reads the runner's history<Cite n="2" />. Before any plan exists it answers one question honestly: is this goal realistic from where you are now? If it isn't, it says what would need to change first.

- **A plan with phases and rules.** Base, build, peak and taper, with hard limits on weekly load, long runs and how hard days are spaced. The limits are enforced by the engine, not suggested.
- **Re-planning after every run.** When a run is missed, the volume is dropped rather than squeezed into the following days. When a runner reports pain, the plan opens a 72-hour protected window with lighter load before rebuilding.
- **An explanation for every change.** Each adjustment comes with the reason behind it, in plain language.

## Why it's built this way

The planner is deterministic: the same inputs always produce the same plan, and every rule can be traced to the code that applies it. A language model reads free text, such as "my calf felt tight today", but it never makes a training decision.

It's the same discipline a good valuation model needs. Assumptions are explicit, the rules can be audited, and the results can be reproduced. When I change a rule, a sweep regenerates 954 plans across every distance and level and shows what moved<Cite n="3" />.
