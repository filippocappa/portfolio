## Why I built it

My schedule, email, training and study material lived in separate apps. I wanted one assistant I could talk to that worked on my own data, ran on my own machine, and never acted on its own when it mattered.

## How it works

A Python server runs all the time on a desktop at home. The iPhone app talks to it over a private network, so it's never exposed to the public internet<Cite n="3" />. Behind it, four specialists split the work: a secretary for calendar and email, a professor for coursework, a coach for health and habits, and one for finance.

- **It asks before it acts.** Sending an email or moving an event goes into a queue, and nothing happens until I tap confirm.
- **It studies with me.** It downloads course material from my university's portal, transcribes recorded lectures on the home computer's graphics card, and turns both into notes, flashcards and practice exams.
- **It starts the day.** A morning briefing covers the day's calendar and when I need to leave to be on time.

## Where it is now

It's in personal use and still moving fast: 101 commits in its first five days<Cite n="1" />. The newest pieces are a study tab and practice exams.
